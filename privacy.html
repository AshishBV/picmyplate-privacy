PicMyPlate

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 7 October 2026

PicMyPlate is a calorie and nutrition tracker for Android. This policy explains exactly what the app collects, where it goes, and how to get rid of it. It is written to be read, not to be survived.

The short version. You need an account to use PicMyPlate. What you log — meals and their photos, weights, progress photos, your profile and goals — is kept on your phone and copied to our server so it survives a lost phone. Photos you scan, voice clips you record, and some of what you type or have logged, are sent to an AI provider to be analysed; a voice clip is never kept. If you join a family group, the other members can see your daily calorie total, your target and your streak, and you can message each other — but never your weight. Health Connect data is read on your phone and never uploaded, except smart-scale weigh-ins, which join your weight log and are backed up with it. The Premium blood sugar log — readings, medicines and their reminders — stays on your phone and is never uploaded. We do not sell your data and we do not use it for advertising.

1. Who we are

PicMyPlate is operated by an individual developer based in India. For any privacy question, correction or data request, contact support@picmyplate.app. We aim to respond within 30 days.

Blood sugar log (Premium)

The blood sugar log is a Premium feature, and what goes into it stays on your phone: the readings you enter, the HbA1c results you type in from a lab report, your medicines (a name, the times, and whether it is insulin) with the doses you tick as taken, and your settings for them. The type of diabetes you can pick in setup — an optional question, asked on every plan — is kept the same way. None of it is copied to our server, sent to an AI provider or shared with a family group. We hold no copy, so we cannot see, restore or export it for you. Signing out or deleting your account erases it from the phone and cancels its reminders.

You can switch on three reminders: a daily fasting check, one two hours after a meal you log, and one at each time you set for a medicine. They are scheduled on your phone and send nothing to us. The app asks Android to keep their text hidden on the lock screen until you unlock the phone; their title is only “Reminder”, and a medicine reminder's text does name the medicine.

The log records and charts what you enter. It does not diagnose or treat diabetes, give dosing advice or replace your doctor — see the Terms.

What the app does not collect

  • No location data of any kind. (The app tailors food to a country — the one you choose, or, until you do, the one your phone's time zone or language setting suggests. It never reads GPS or network location.)
  • No contacts, call logs, installed-app lists, or any of your phone's own messages. (The app has its own family chat — that is described in section 7 — but it cannot see your SMS or any other messaging app.)
  • No advertising identifier, and no advertising or tracking SDKs are present in the app.
  • No usage analytics. The app sends no analytics events or usage statistics of any kind. The only diagnostic data it sends is the crash reports described in section 12.

3. Your account

An account is required. Sign up with an email address and password, or with Google. Everything you log is saved on your phone first, so the app keeps working offline, and is copied to our server whenever you are online so it survives a lost or replaced phone.

Signing out removes your account's data from the phone. What was copied to our server stays there until you delete it or delete your account; water, fasting, hand-logged exercise, Quick-add favourites and the blood sugar log are never copied, so signing out removes them for good.

4. Food photos and AI

When you scan a meal, a nutrition label or the ingredients you have, the photograph is sent through our own server to an AI provider to be analysed. The request goes through our server so that our API keys are not exposed inside the app. We use several providers so the app keeps working when one is unavailable, and which one answers can change from request to request: Groq, Google (the Gemini API), Cerebras (text only) and OpenRouter, which passes the request on to the maker of the model it uses — currently Google or OpenAI. The result comes straight back to you. Our server does not keep a scanned photo once the answer is returned; if you log the meal, its photo is saved with it (section 2).

To make a meal scan more accurate, the request also carries your country (see "What the app does not collect") and up to six dishes you corrected before, with the calories you gave them.

Dish names, on the AI Insights screen

Photos are not the only thing that goes to an AI provider, and this is the other one. When you open AI Insights and have logged at least six meals, the app sends the names of dishes you have logged — up to fifty entries, each a dish name with the hour of day it was eaten — through our server to the same providers, and asks which meal of the day each one belongs to. It is what lets the screen say how many of your days have a breakfast, a lunch or a dinner recorded.

What is sent is only the dish name and an hour, for example "Paneer tikka" at 20. No calories, no macros, no photographs, no weight, no dates, and nothing that identifies you — not your name, your email or your account id. The answer is held only in the app's memory, for up to a day, so opening the screen again does not send it again; if the app has been closed, or you have logged a new dish, the next visit sends it again.

The same caution as above applies, for the same reason: once a dish name reaches an AI provider it is governed by that provider's terms, not ours. If you would rather this did not happen, do not open the AI Insights screen — it is the only place that sends this list.

Text you type, meal plans and recipes

Photos and dish names are not the only things that reach the provider. Any text you type that asks the app to look something up goes the same way: the food description you write instead of scanning, and the dish name you ask for nutrition on. It is sent to answer that request, and the answer comes straight back to you. As with a photograph, do not type anything into those boxes that you would not want a third party to read.

When you generate a meal plan, the request carries what the plan has to fit: your goal, your calorie, protein, carbohydrate and fat targets, your current and target weight, your dietary preference (in a family, the strictest one in the group), your country, the ingredients you entered if you asked to cook from what you have, and the names of dishes recent plans used so they are not repeated. A recipe request carries the dish, your dietary preference, goal and country, and the calories and protein it should come to. Neither carries your name, your email or your account id.

A dish you ask nutrition for may first be looked up in the USDA FoodData Central database. A dish's name may be used to find a stock photo on Pexels or Unsplash (through our server) or on TheMealDB (asked directly by your phone); the picture is then loaded from that service.

Voice input and the microphone

In Describe Meal and Search food you can tap the microphone and say what you ate instead of typing it. The app asks for Android's microphone permission the first time, and uses the microphone only while you have asked it to listen: from your tap until you tap again, 15 seconds at most. It stops as soon as you leave the screen or switch to another app.

The recording — a voice clip of a few seconds — is sent through our server to be transcribed into text by Groq (its Whisper speech model), or by Google (the Gemini API) if Groq is unavailable. The text comes back into the box, where you can change it before anything is estimated or logged. The clip is not stored by us — not on our server and not in our logs — and it is deleted from your phone once it has been sent. We keep only a daily count of voice requests, to enforce a fair-use limit.

As with a photo, once a clip reaches the provider it is governed by that provider's terms, not ours, so say only what you ate. If you would rather not use voice, type instead, or refuse or revoke the microphone permission in your phone's settings; nothing else in the app uses the microphone.

Please read this part. Once a photo or a voice clip reaches an AI provider it is governed by that provider's terms, not ours. Google states that content submitted through the free tier of its Gemini API may be used to improve its services, including review by human reviewers — and Gemini is also the fallback that transcribes a voice clip when Groq is unavailable. Do not photograph anything you would not want a third party to see — documents, faces, screens or other people — and photograph the food only. In a voice clip, say only what you ate.

Provider terms: Google Gemini API · Groq · Cerebras · OpenRouter.

5. Health Connect

If you connect Health Connect (Settings → Exercise calories → Connect Health Connect, or when the app asks during setup) and allow access, the app reads the data below from Android Health Connect for the days you view. Your steps, exercise and sleep are shown back to you on the Progress screen, and if you also switch on "Add exercise calories", the calories you burned are added to that day's calorie budget on your own screen. Weigh-ins from a smart scale are added to your weight log. It reads seven things:

  • Active calories burned
  • Total calories burned
  • Steps
  • Distance
  • Exercise sessions — the start time, end time and activity type of workouts other apps have recorded
  • Sleep sessions — the start time and end time of sleep other apps or a watch have recorded, and where those records include them, the sleep stages within a night (deep, light, REM and time awake)
  • Weight — weigh-ins a smart scale or another app has recorded. The app asks for the last 90 days, but Health Connect only shares data from about 30 days before you first allowed access unless an app also holds its separate history permission, which PicMyPlate does not request — so a recent connection may bring in less than 90 days. The first weigh-in of each day is added to your weight log, marked with the app it came from. A weight you typed yourself for that day is kept and never replaced.
  • Everything except weight is read on your device and used immediately. It is not uploaded to our server, not stored by us, and not shared with anyone — including the other members of a family group.
  • An imported weigh-in becomes an entry in your weight log, and is stored like a weight you typed: on your device and on our server with the rest of your log, so it survives a lost phone (see section 2). Like every weight, it is never shared with a family group. Deleting your account deletes it.
  • It is never used for advertising, and never sold or transferred.
  • The app reads only. It never writes to Health Connect.
  • You can revoke access at any time in the Health Connect app, which stops all seven. Weigh-ins already added to your log stay there, as entries you can type over. Switching off "Add exercise calories" in PicMyPlate only stops burned calories being added to your budget.

Our use of Health Connect complies with the Google Play Health Apps policy, including its restrictions on the use and transfer of health data.

6. Nutrition corrections are pooled

When you correct a food's nutrition values, that correction is stored against your account and also contributes to a shared, aggregated estimate for that dish. Once enough different people have corrected the same dish and broadly agree, the improved value is used for everyone. This is how the app gets better at regional foods that no database covers well.

What is shared is the dish name and the corrected nutrition numbers, combined statistically with other people's. Your identity is not attached to the pooled value and it is not shown to other users. The pooled value is recalculated from the individual corrections each time it is used, so deleting your account removes your contribution.

7. Family groups, chat and shared progress

Family groups are opt-in. You are only in one if you created it or entered someone's share code, and you can leave at any time. Leaving stops all further sharing immediately.

What the other members of your group can see:

  • The display name and the emoji or profile photo you chose for the group.
  • Your calorie target, your goal (lose, maintain or gain), your dietary preference and how many meals a day you eat, so the shared meal plan can be portioned for each person.
  • Your calorie total for the day, whether it is within 10% of your target, and how many days in a row you have logged.
  • Reactions you leave on their day, and anything you write in the group chat.

Your weight is never shared. Nor is anything else in this list: your individual meals, your food photos, your progress photos, your water intake, your email address, or anything read from Health Connect. None of it is sent to the group, and your weight is not stored on the family side of our database at all.

This is not something you can partly opt out of. Being in a family group means sharing those numbers with the people in it. If you would rather not share them, do not join a group, or leave the one you are in.

Profile photos

You can set a picture from your gallery instead of an emoji. It is stored in a private folder, not a public one: it has no address anyone can open, and it is served through a temporary link that is issued only to someone who is in a family with you at the moment they load it. Leave the group and no new link is issued to them; a link they already hold stops working within 12 hours. There is no permanent URL in circulation.

Replacing your picture deletes the previous one. Choosing an emoji instead removes the photo entirely. We never look at these pictures, and they are never sent to an AI provider.

Messages

Group messages are stored as ordinary text on our server, so that they reach the other members and so that we can look at one that somebody reports. They are not end-to-end encrypted: content nobody can read is content nobody can moderate.

Messages are deleted automatically 90 days after they are sent, whether or not anyone deletes them. You can delete your own at any time by holding a message. Leaving a group stops further sharing but does not retroactively remove messages you have already sent; those are covered by the 90-day rule.

Reporting and blocking

Hold someone else's message to report it, or to block the person who sent it.

  • Reporting sends us a copy of the message, who sent it, and the reason you chose. We keep that copy even if the message is later deleted — otherwise anyone reported could simply delete the evidence. We review reports and may remove content or suspend accounts.
  • Blocking stops that person's messages from being sent to your device at all; it is enforced on our server, not hidden in the app. It is one-directional and private: they are not told, and they stay in the family group.

8. Subscriptions

Paid plans are billed by Google Play. We never see or handle your card, bank or payment details — Google does not share them with us. The app asks Google Play which subscriptions are active on the device's Play account and unlocks features accordingly. Manage or cancel a subscription in the Play Store under Subscriptions.

What we keep about a purchase. So that our server knows which plan your account is on, the app sends it the purchase token Google Play issued for your subscription: when you buy, when you restore purchases, and when you open the app if that subscription was last checked more than six hours earlier. Our server asks Google Play about the token and stores, against your account, the plan (Pro or Premium), the product, the token, the subscription's status according to Google Play (for example active, on hold, cancelled or expired), the date the subscription runs until and when it was last checked — and nothing else about the purchase. Our server may also ask Google Play about the token again on its own, at most once a day, so that a refund or a cancellation takes effect even if you do not open the app; it sends Google Play only the token. The token is kept so that one purchase can belong to only one account; when you buy, the app also tags the purchase with your account id, a random identifier and not your email, for the same reason. Our server decides your plan, and the limits that go with it, from this record and not from the plan the app reports with each scan. The record is not removed when a subscription ends or is cancelled — it simply carries its expiry date — and is deleted when you delete your account (section 11).

9. Notifications

Reminders are optional and are generated on your device. Enabling them does not send anything to us. The Premium blood sugar and medicine reminders are described under “Blood sugar log (Premium)” in section 2.

10. Who we share data with

We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it for advertising. Data is shared only with the service providers needed to run the app:

  • Supabase — accounts, database, photo storage, and the server functions that relay AI requests.
  • Groq, Google (Gemini API), Cerebras and OpenRouter, and the model makers OpenRouter passes requests to (currently Google and OpenAI) — photo and text recognition, voice transcription (Groq, with Google as the fallback), meal plans and recipes, as described in section 4.
  • USDA FoodData Central, Pexels and Unsplash — dish names, through our server, to look up nutrition or a stock photo.
  • Open Food Facts — barcode numbers, and the words you type in Search food to find packaged products, from your phone.
  • TheMealDB — recipe names, from your phone, to find a photo.
  • Brevo — sends account emails.
  • Google Play — subscription billing; our server also sends it a purchase token to confirm a subscription (section 8).
  • Google Sign-In — only if you choose it, to verify who you are.
  • Expo — delivers app updates; your phone contacts Expo's servers to check for them.

Some of these providers process data outside the country you live in, including in the United States.

Separately from service providers: if you are in a family group, the other members of that group see the things listed in section 7. That is sharing with other people rather than with a company, it happens only because you joined a group, and it stops when you leave.

We may also disclose data where required by law.

11. How long we keep it

Data linked to your account is kept until you delete it or delete your account. Deleting your account removes your account, your logged meals and their photos, weights and notes, progress photos, profile, goals, custom foods, your family profile photo, your family messages, your shared daily numbers, reactions you left and reactions on your day, reports you filed, people you blocked, your nutrition corrections, your AI usage counts, your crash reports and your subscription record (section 8). Backups may retain copies for up to 30 days before being overwritten.

A voice clip is never kept: it is deleted from your phone once sent, and our server does not store it at all, so there is nothing of it to delete.

A meal you delete stays in Recently deleted on your phone for 30 days so you can restore it. It leaves our server at the next sync, and only its random id is kept so your other devices know it was deleted. The photo file of a deleted meal or progress photo stays in your private storage folder until you delete your account.

Family messages are deleted 90 days after they are sent, regardless of whether the account still exists. Reports are kept for as long as we need them to act on the account they concern, including a copy of a message someone else reported. Crash reports are kept until you delete your account. The hashed device identifier is not stored against your account and is kept so the scan count cannot be reset by reinstalling.

12. Security

Traffic is encrypted in transit. Every database table uses row-level security so that your rows can only be read by your own account, or — for a family group — by the members of that group. Passwords are hashed by our authentication provider. No system is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

Crash reports. If the app crashes, it saves a report on your device and uploads it the next time you open the app, so the fault can be found and fixed. A report contains the error, where in the code it happened, and the app and Android versions, and is linked to your account. Personal values are stripped before it is sent: no meals, no photos, no messages, no email address.

Report a problem. If you use Settings → Report a problem, your email app opens a message to us with the app and Android versions and recent error messages filled in. Nothing is sent until you send it.

13. Your rights and how to delete your data

  • In the app: Settings → Account → Delete account & data. This removes your account and its stored data permanently.
  • Without the app: see the account deletion page.
  • Access or correction: email support@picmyplate.app and we will provide a copy of your stored data or correct it. Your blood sugar log is not part of that: it is only on your phone.
  • Withdrawing consent: revoke Health Connect access, revoke the microphone permission, leave a family group, or delete your account.
  • Uninstalling removes everything held on the device.

If you are in India, you have rights under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. If you are in the EEA or UK, you have rights under the GDPR, including access, correction, erasure, portability and objection. Use the contact address above to exercise any of them.

14. Children

PicMyPlate is not intended for children under 13, and is not directed at them. Calorie targets are calculated with formulas intended for adults. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we will delete it.
